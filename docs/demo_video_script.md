# Demo video script (target: 3 min 30 s)

**Setup before recording:** run `streamlit run app.py`, open the app full-screen at 100% zoom with the default filters (2016–2025, all of France, overseas on, *Sexual violence*). Record the screen with the microphone on (OBS, Loom or the Windows Game Bar).

| Time | On screen / action | Voice-over (read naturally) |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Title and "Why it matters" | "Hi, I'm [name]. Every year French police and gendarmerie record about 3.6 million offences. People often ask whether crime is going up. My app shows that this is the wrong question, because the *kind* of crime being recorded has changed. The audience is local decision-makers and journalists." |
| 0:20–0:35 | Hover the yellow warning box | "One caveat first: this is *recorded* crime. It depends on whether victims go to the police. Only about 12% of victims of sexual violence do, against 74% for burglary." |
| 0:35–1:10 | Scroll to **The big picture**. Point at the KPI row, then hover the index chart (2020 dip, 2025 end values) | "The data come from the Interior Ministry's statistical service on data.gouv.fr: 101 départements, 10 years, 18 indicators. Since 2016, recorded violence against persons is up 84% while property crime is down 14%. Every family dips in 2020 with the lockdowns. Violence rises fastest after #MeToo and after the 2019 Grenelle on domestic violence." |
| 1:10–1:25 | Hover the red and blue bar chart | "By indicator: sexual violence +148% and domestic violence +120%, while violent theft halves and vehicle theft falls by a quarter." |
| 1:25–1:55 | **Where and who**: show the map, click *Change since start year*, hover two départements | "Where does this happen? Everywhere. The sexual-violence rate rose in all 101 départements. Paris has the highest level. In percentage terms, rural départements like the Hautes-Alpes grew fastest from a low base." |
| 1:55–2:15 | Scroll the small multiples; in the sidebar change the indicator to *Residential burglary* and show the map change | "The regional panels share one axis, so levels are comparable. If I switch the focus to burglary, the picture flips: it fell in two-thirds of départements. Every chart and KPI follows the sidebar filters." |
| 2:15–2:40 | Switch back to *Sexual violence*. Move the Top-N slider; hover the box plot and the scatter | "Rankings and distribution: the top 8 and bottom 8 départements, the spread each year, and the size effect. Population barely explains the rate, r = −0.12. Paris and the two Corsican départements come out as statistical outliers." |
| 2:40–3:05 | **What if reporting changed?** Drag the reporting slider to +100%, then to +155% | "So is this more violence or more reporting? If the share of victims who report doubled, about 27% of the rise is still left over. Reporting would need to rise by 155% to explain all of it. The data can't settle this on its own, so the assumption is the user's to set." |
| 3:05–3:20 | **Data quality**: show the checks table and the sparsity heatmap | "On data quality: no missing values or duplicates, a balanced panel, and rates that I recompute and check against the published ones. The one warning is that the latest years use 2023 population figures." |
| 3:20–3:35 | **Takeaways** | "Takeaways: plan for more victims coming forward, never report crime as a single number, and add victimisation surveys as a next step. Thanks for watching." |

**Tips:** move the mouse slowly; let each hover tooltip show for about a second. Keep the video under 4 minutes. Export as MP4 at 1080p.
